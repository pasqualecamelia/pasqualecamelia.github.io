02What the transform is, and what it is not

The FMT is a 10 × 10 Vandermonde frame sampled on the golden scale lattice: frequencies ωj = φ(j−5)/5 against log-coordinates uk = (k/5)·log φ. It is not unitary, and Parseval fails on it.

The non-unitarity is not inherited from the Mellin transform. The continuous Mellin–Plancherel transform is unitary: with (𝒲f)(u) = f(eu) one has ‖𝒲f‖L²(ℝ,du) = ‖f‖L²(ℝ₊,dx/x), so ℳ = ℱc𝒲 is a composition of two unitaries, and it conjugates the dilation generator −i·x d/dx into −i·d/du. Multiplicative structure by itself loses nothing.

The deficit comes from the finite construction, and from three features of it: the sampling is finite (N = 10), the frequencies are geometric rather than equispaced, and the resulting Vandermonde nodes are non-orthogonal — they sit in an arc of 4.70° on the unit circle.

What the frame does give is a gain-invariant readout: under f ↦ λf the ratio between the two frequency sheets is unchanged, because both scale by |λ|. That ratio, not the energy, is the quantity the tools report. No claim of superiority over Fourier is made anywhere — the two answer different questions.

04What double precision cannot do — and what it still can

With κ ≈ 4.7 × 1018, sixteen significant digits are not enough to resolve the bottom of the spectrum. Double precision returns σmin of order 10−17 and κ of order 1017: wrong by one to two orders of magnitude, and the exact figures differ between machines because they depend on the linear-algebra library.

The same conditioning governs the polar factor. For a non-singular matrix the unitary polar factor is unique and obeys

‖U(A+E) − U(A)‖_F  ≤  2‖E‖_F / (σ_min(A) + σ_min(A+E))

Building the matrix in double precision perturbs it by ‖E‖F ≈ 4 × 10−16, so the bound is of order 103: no control at all. Measured against the 120-digit factor, three admissible float64 constructions of the same matrix, on two independent machines, give deviations spread over

0.5  ≤  ‖ΔU_φ‖_F   ≤  3
0.05 ≤  ‖ΔP_χ‖_F   ≤  1

— O(1), and not reproducible. That is why neither the paper nor this page pins a single value: the spread is the phenomenon. Restricted to the five retained singular directions the two factors agree to ≤ 10−10: what double precision loses is the orientation of the ill-conditioned complement, not the block the readout actually uses.

What survives at any precision. The coisometry identities RR* = I₂, rank Pχ = 2, dim ker Rχ = 8 hold for any unitary, so they are satisfied by whichever factor your machine computes: the measured defect is ≈ 5 × 10−16 in double precision and ≈ 10−120 at 120 digits. A theorem about all unitaries and a certified statement about one specific matrix are different objects, and only the second needs multiprecision.

The Console reports both columns side by side: what it computed here, and what the certified record says. Any disagreement you see in the last digits is the point being made, not a malfunction.

05Run it locally

git clone https://github.com/pasqualecamelia/qfmt-research-simulator
cd qfmt-research-simulator
make dev        # editable install with test extras
make test       # 25 tests
make certify    # multiprecision certification, 40–120 digits, JSON output
make quick      # full quick profile into outputs/quick

The package separates three levels that should not be conflated: the reproducible FMT core, the quantum numerical constructions built on the polar factor, and the exploratory benchmarks. Each carries its own status in the documentation; the validation report states which numbers are certified and which are illustrative.
